# Teaching and assessment approach

## My instructional role

I designed the independent-study assignments and supervised students working through the lab's simulated SOC workflows. The assignment book requires analysis notes, evidence exports, case screenshots, report submissions, and knowledge-check responses. Students must explain why their findings support a classification or response recommendation.

## Selected examples

In Week 12, students collect and compare Windows and Linux evidence, reconstruct activity, and connect endpoint findings with alerts. In Week 13, students compare Caldera execution with Wazuh telemetry, identify activities that did or did not generate an alert, and document a SOC case.

The public adaptations preserve those objectives while removing access details and correcting overly broad technique mappings.

## Suggested rubric for the public samples

This rubric is a newly proposed portfolio aid, not a claim about grades or weights used in prior semesters.

| Criterion | Suggested weight | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence handling and traceability | 25% | Source references, original copies, hash inventory, collection scope |
| Analysis and correlation | 30% | Timeline, endpoint/time matches, alternative explanations |
| ATT&CK mapping and detection reasoning | 20% | Behavior-specific mappings, coverage limits, justified gaps |
| Response judgment | 15% | Proportionate next actions and stated uncertainty |
| Communication | 10% | Clear case summary, readable exhibits, concise report |

## Feedback workflow for reuse

A reviewer can identify the claim, inspect the cited evidence, request clarification when evidence is insufficient, and ask the student to revise the report. These are recommended feedback steps for the public samples; a separate historical assessment record would be needed to document specific past feedback or measured learning gains.

A lighter entry route uses the synthetic CSV/JSON fixtures. A more advanced route repeats the collection in an authorized lab and compares actual telemetry against expected behavior. Both require the student to distinguish observations from inference.
